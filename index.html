Oil Painting is one of the oldest and most famous painting techniques in the world. Oil paints use pigments mixed with drying oils, allowing artists to create rich colours, smooth blending, and detailed textures. One of the world's most famous oil paintings is The Starry Night, painted by Vincent van Gogh in 1889. Van Gogh was a Dutch artist, and the painting was created while he was staying at an asylum in Saint-Rémy, France. The swirling sky and bright stars make this painting instantly recognizable. Today, The Starry Night is preserved at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York and is considered one of the most iconic works of modern art.

Watercolor Painting is a technique that uses pigments mixed with water and is usually painted on paper. It is known for its transparent colours, soft washes, and ability to create beautiful effects of light. One famous watercolor artwork is The Blue Rigi, Sunrise, painted by J.M.W. Turner in 1842. Turner was a famous British artist who became especially well known for his landscape paintings. The painting shows Mount Rigi in Switzerland during sunrise, with delicate layers of colour creating a misty atmosphere. Turner's watercolors helped demonstrate that watercolor could be an important and highly respected form of fine art.

Acrylic Painting is a relatively modern technique that uses fast-drying acrylic paint. It became popular among artists during the 20th century because it can produce bright colours and both smooth and textured effects. One of the most famous acrylic paintings is A Bigger Splash, created by David Hockney in 1967. Hockney was a British artist who became famous for his paintings of California's bright sunlight and swimming pools. The painting shows a large splash in a swimming pool, while the person who caused it is not visible. Hockney used a paint roller for large flat areas and small brushes to carefully create the splash, which took about two weeks to paint.

Pencil Art is a drawing technique that uses graphite, charcoal, or other dry materials to create images on paper. Artists can create light and dark shades, fine lines, textures, and realistic details by changing the pressure and direction of the pencil. One famous early example of detailed pencil-like drawing is Self-Portrait, created by Albrecht Dürer in 1484. Dürer was a famous German artist and one of the most important artists of the Northern Renaissance. The original work was created using silverpoint, an early drawing technique similar to the detailed line work later associated with pencil art. It is one of Dürer's earliest known self-portraits and is preserved at the Albertina Museum in Vienna, Austria.
